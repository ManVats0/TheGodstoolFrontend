Data cleaning guide

A CSV data cleaning checklist: ten checks, in the right order

The order matters because early mistakes hide later ones. Fix the file's encoding and structure first, then the types, then the values, and finish by proving that nothing was lost. Each step below says what to look for, the trap that catches people, and where a deeper guide exists.

Updated 4 November 2026. Examples on this page were run for real; the data in them is made up.

The checklist

1. Keep an untouched copy

Do every change on a copy. Write down the row count and the total of any money column now. You will need both at step 10.

2. Check the encoding and the delimiter

If names show as Ã© instead of é, the file is UTF-8 but was read as something else. Re-import choosing UTF-8 rather than repairing the text by hand. In many European settings a "CSV" is separated by semicolons, because the comma is the decimal mark. If everything lands in one column, that is why.

3. Check the structure

One header row, the same number of columns on every row, no merged cells, no totals row at the bottom, no blank rows in the middle. A quoted field with a line break inside can make one record look like two.

4. Strip invisible whitespace

Trim leading and trailing spaces, collapse doubled spaces, and remove non-breaking spaces. Excel's TRIM does not remove CHAR(160). This one step fixes a surprising share of "duplicates" and failed lookups.

5. Fix the types

Numbers stored as text, text that should stay text (postal codes, phone numbers, IDs with leading zeros). See numbers stored as text, N/A and blanks.

6. Fix the dates

Find the day and month order from the file itself, then write everything as YYYY-MM-DD. See mixed and ambiguous dates. Do this before deduplication, because the same record with two date formats will not match.

7. Decide what blank means

Empty, N/A, null, -, unknown: choose one way to write "not recorded" and use it everywhere. Count the blanks per column. A column that is 90 percent blank is usually a problem upstream, not something to fill in.

8. Standardise the text values

Country names, statuses, categories. List the distinct values of each column and look for variants such as UK, U.K. and United Kingdom. Map them to one spelling and keep the mapping, so you can apply it again next month.

9. Remove duplicates, carefully

Do it after steps 4 to 8, so the text is comparable. Decide whether rows are records or transactions first. See duplicate customers, including the misspelled ones.

10. Reconcile

Compare to the numbers you wrote down in step 1. Rows after = rows before minus rows you meant to remove. The money total should match unless you removed or corrected specific rows, and you can name them. If you cannot explain a difference, do not send the file.

Range and sense checks

Alongside step 7, look at the smallest and largest value in each numeric and date column. A birth date in the future, a negative quantity, a price of 0.01 or 1,000,000 are quicker to spot as the min and max than by scrolling.

For bank statements, a stronger check exists

A statement prints a running balance, so each row can be tested against the one before it. That is a proof rather than a sense check. See bank statement PDF to Excel, every row checked.

Questions people ask

What order should I clean a CSV in?

Encoding and structure first, then whitespace, types, dates, blanks and text values, then duplicates, and finish by reconciling row counts and totals against the original.

Why clean dates before removing duplicates?

The same record written with two date formats will not match itself, so it survives deduplication. Normalise values first, then compare.

How do I know I did not lose data?

Write down the row count and the totals of key columns before you start, and compare afterwards. Every difference should be explained by a change you made on purpose.

Why does my CSV show strange characters?

The file's encoding does not match how it was read. Re-import and choose UTF-8 instead of editing the garbled text by hand.
